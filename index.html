How the numbers are built

Everything here is a starting point for training, not a prescription. Try race-day amounts in training first, and adjust based on how you feel and on your sweat tests.

Carbs during a session

Based on Jeukendrup's duration-based guidelines (Sports Medicine, 2014). Within each band, the target moves toward the top as average intensity and time above threshold go up. It's capped by your gut tolerance.

DurationCarbs per hour
Under 45 minNone needed
45–75 min0–30 g (small amounts or a mouth rinse on hard sessions)
75–150 min30–60 g
Over 150 min60–90 g, up to 120 g if your gut is trained for it

Above 60 g/h the mix recipe switches to glucose (maltodextrin) plus fructose at about 1 : 0.8. Your gut absorbs the two through different routes, so you can take in more.

Daily carbs

From the ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada joint position on nutrition and athletic performance (Thomas, Erdman & Burke, 2016). Protein is set at 1.4–1.6 g/kg (higher on strength days), inside their 1.2–2.0 g/kg range.

Sweat rate

Your baseline, from the 1–4 description, a number you know, or your logged tests, is for a steady run in mild weather. It's then scaled by:

IntensityZ1 ×0.8 · Z2 ×1.0 · Z3 ×1.15 · Z4 ×1.3 · Z5 ×1.4, time-weighted across your blocks
Temperature+2.5% per °F above 65°F (−2.5% below), held between ×0.6 and ×1.8
HumidityDry ×0.95 · Normal ×1.0 · Humid ×1.15
SportRun ×1.0 · Outdoor bike ×0.85 (airflow) · Indoor trainer ×1.2 · Hyrox ×0.95 · Other ×0.9 · Strength ×0.6 · Swim ×0.5

These multipliers are reasonable rules of thumb, not published constants. Your own sweat tests are the best correction.

The 1–4 descriptions map to about 0.5, 0.8, 1.2 and 1.6 L/h. Most endurance athletes measure between about 0.5 and 2.0 L/h (Baker, Sports Medicine, 2017).

Fluid

Sessions under an hour: drink to thirst. Longer: aim to replace about 70% of sweat loss, at 0.4–1.0 L/h, and never more than you sweat. Drinking past your sweat rate is how hyponatremia (low blood sodium) happens. The planner flags any session projected to cost more than 2% of body weight, the point where performance tends to drop (ACSM fluid replacement position stand, 2007).

Sodium

Sweat sodium varies a lot from person to person. In Baker's 2017 review, average concentrations ranged from about 230 to over 2,000 mg/L, around a mean near 950. The 1–5 saltiness scale maps to 400, 650, 950, 1,250 and 1,600 mg/L. A lab result replaces it.

Sodium lost per hour = sweat rate × concentration. The planner suggests replacing about half of that in sessions of 90 min to 3 h, and about three-quarters beyond 3 h. That works out to 200–1,500 mg/h. Short sessions just need a salted meal afterward, unless it's hot.

Table salt is about 39% sodium by weight, so 1 g of salt has about 390 mg of sodium.

Zones

Joe Friel-style run zones. HR as % of LTHR: Z1 under 85, Z2 85–89, Z3 90–94, Z4 95–99, Z5 100+. Pace as a multiple of threshold pace: Z1 slower than 1.29×, Z2 1.14–1.29×, Z3 1.06–1.13×, Z4 0.99–1.05×, Z5 faster.
